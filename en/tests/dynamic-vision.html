Dynamic Vision Test guide

What is dynamic vision?

It's the ability to instantly catch and recognize something that's moving or appears and vanishes quickly. This test measures how fast you can read information within a very short exposure window rather than actual motion, but the underlying skill is similar. It's a big factor when tracking a fast ball in sports like soccer or baseball, or spotting an enemy that flashes across your screen for a split second in an FPS game.

What's average, and what's a good score?

On this site's scale, reading digits at 200ms or less is good, 130ms or less is excellent, 80ms or less is top tier, and 50ms or less is pro-gamer level. These are our own grade bands, not official statistics.

The shorter the exposure, the more your monitor's refresh rate matters. On a 60Hz screen one frame is about 16.7ms, so around 50ms device differences feed straight into the result.

How to measure accurately

  • Rest your gaze where the digit will appear and wait — don't spend time searching for it.
  • The higher your monitor's refresh rate, the more faithfully a short flash is actually shown.
  • A very dark room or an overly bright screen causes after-images that get in the way.
  • Repeat several times and treat the exposure you read reliably as your record.

Can dynamic vision be trained?

Dynamic vision is partly natural, but it's also believed to improve somewhat through repeated visual training and experience with games and sports. That said, results here depend on your screen's refresh rate, device performance, and how alert you are — so treat it as a fun estimate.
