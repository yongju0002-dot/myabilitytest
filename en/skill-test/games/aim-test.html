What is "aim"? It's made up of two components: "flicking" — snapping quickly and precisely onto a stationary target — and "tracking" — following a moving target while keeping your crosshair on it. This test measures flicking. Reaction speed alone isn't the full picture; how precisely you hit the center of the target matters just as much for real aim skill.

Tuning your mouse DPI and in-game sensitivity to match your hand movement matters too. Too high and you'll be fast but sloppy; too low and you'll be precise but slow. Consistent practice with an aim trainer is generally believed to improve both speed and accuracy over time.
