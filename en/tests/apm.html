APM Test guide

What is APM (Actions Per Minute)?

It's the number of actions — mouse clicks plus key presses — you perform in one minute. It became widely known as a measure of pro-player skill in StarCraft, and it's still used as a yardstick for control speed in RTS and MOBA games. StarCraft pros are generally reported to average somewhere around 300–400 APM during matches.

What's average, and what's a good score?

On this site's scale, 60–110 APM is average (C), 110–160 is above average, and over 220 is very fast. StarCraft pros are widely reported to play at roughly 300–400 APM, so 300+ is graded as pro level.

Note that APM here is raw input speed, not meaningful in-game actions, so the same person can score differently from their real game APM.

How to measure accurately

  • Keep the pace for the full 60 seconds — a fast start and a slow finish lowers the average.
  • Use the keyboard and the mouse; both count as actions.
  • Holding a key down doesn't auto-count. Each press-and-release is one action.
  • Check the 10-second chart to see where you slow down.

Why high APM isn't the whole story

That said, high APM doesn't automatically mean high skill. Meaningless repeated inputs inflate the number just as easily, so what really matters is the share of actions that accomplish something. This test graphs your APM in 10-second segments, so you can also see whether your speed fades toward the end.
