What is a reaction time test?

A reaction time test measures, in milliseconds (ms), how quickly you click after visually noticing a color change on screen. The lower the number, the faster your reaction speed. It's a common indicator of split-second responsiveness used in gaming and sports.

What's the average reaction time in ms?

Visual reaction time typically falls in the 250–300ms range for most people. Low 200s is faster than average, and 150–200ms is a well-trained level. Results can shift by 10–50ms depending on your device, monitor refresh rate, and touchscreen latency.

How to measure accurately

A single try is less reliable than an average of 5+ tries. Cold hands, drowsiness, wireless mice, or mobile touch input can all add extra delay — repeat under the same device/environment to compare fairly. Clicking before the color actually changes won't give you a true reaction time reading.

How is the percentile calculated?

The "top X%" figure on the results screen is an estimated curve built around the ~273ms median that's widely reported for online reaction-time tests, shaped to match the right-skewed pattern commonly observed in that data (a fast-side cluster around 220–320ms with a long slow-side tail). It isn't a published table from a single study, and real-world results vary with momentary condition and device setup.

References: Der, G., & Deary, I. J. (2006). Age and sex differences in reaction time in adulthood. Psychology and Aging, 21(1), 62–73 (UK Health & Lifestyle Survey, n=7,130). Talboom, J. S., et al. (2021). MindCrowd, a large-scale online cognitive assessment cohort measuring reaction time and related measures. npj Aging and Mechanisms of Disease (n=75,666). Both support the general finding that adult reaction times cluster in the 200–300ms range, but neither publishes the exact percentile curve used on this page.
