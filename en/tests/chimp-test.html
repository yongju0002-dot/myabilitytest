What was the Ayumu experiment?

A team led by Tetsuro Matsuzawa at Kyoto University's Primate Research Institute ran a working-memory experiment on a chimpanzee named Ayumu. Numbers 1 through 9 briefly appeared on screen, then were covered by white squares — and Ayumu tapped their locations in the correct order, often faster and more accurately than trained human university students, which made headlines at the time.

What is working memory?

The experiment is often cited as a striking example of how working memory — the ability to briefly hold and manipulate information you've just seen — can differ between humans and chimpanzees. This kind of momentary memory shows up everywhere in daily life, from mental math to conversation to learning.
