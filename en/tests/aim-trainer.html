Aim Test guide

What is "aim"?

It's made up of two components: "flicking" — snapping quickly and precisely onto a stationary target — and "tracking" — following a moving target while keeping your crosshair on it. This test measures flicking. Reaction speed alone isn't the full picture; how precisely you hit the center of the target matters just as much for real aim skill.

What's average, and what's a good score?

On this site's own scale, 450–600ms per target is the typical range; 350ms or less is upper tier, and 300ms or less with 90%+ accuracy counts as pro level. These are our grade bands, not official statistics.

Speed alone isn't enough — lots of missed clicks pull your grade down. Real aim is speed and accuracy together, so aim to raise both.

Pro-level requires both speed (300ms or under) and accuracy (90% or higher) at once. Fast clicks that miss the target too often get you Top-tier instead — raw speed with a lot of misses doesn't count for much in real gameplay.

How to measure accurately

  • Keep your usual mouse sensitivity (DPI and in-game sens) while testing.
  • Move with your whole arm, not just the wrist, for steadier large flicks.
  • Judge by the average of several runs, not one.
  • Use the same mouse and sensitivity each time so records are comparable.

Why does mouse setup matter?

Tuning your mouse DPI and in-game sensitivity to match your hand movement matters too. Too high and you'll be fast but sloppy; too low and you'll be precise but slow. Consistent practice with an aim trainer is generally believed to improve both speed and accuracy over time.
